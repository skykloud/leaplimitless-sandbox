The Career Ceiling & Leadership Diagnostic

Identify why your technical excellence isn't converting into executive visibility, board promotions, and upper-quartile compensation.

Executive Evaluation

Question 1 of 6

100% Confidential
Pillar 1: Visibility vs Execution

How do you approach sharing your accomplishments?

In many global cultures, self-promotion feels boastful. In Western executive suites, your visibility determines your opportunities.

I let my results speak for themselves

I rarely bring up my work proactively; I assume my manager and peers notice my technical excellence.

I share updates only during annual reviews

I feel awkward bringing up achievements casually, so I reserve advocacy for formal review cycles.

I strategically broadcast business impact

I regularly tie my team's output to enterprise revenue and ensure C-suite stakeholders hear the story.

Pillar 2: Executive Presence & Voice

How comfortable are you in high-stakes boardroom meetings?

Navigating accent consciousness, deference to authority, and interrupting senior leaders strategically.

I wait to be invited to speak

I focus on taking notes and only share opinions if directly asked by the meeting lead.

I speak, but I over-explain technical minutiae

I worry about being questioned, so I dive into defensive details rather than leading with strategic conclusions.

I frame the strategic synthesis and shape the room

I lead with executive conclusions, synthesize conflicting viewpoints, and command authority effortlessly.

Pillar 3: Cultural Deference & Hierarchy

When your VP or Executive makes a strategic mistake, how do you respond?

Deference to elders and superiors is deeply rooted in many immigrant cultures, but top executives need peers who challenge them constructively.

I stay quiet to avoid disrespecting authority

It feels culturally inappropriate to contradict a superior in public or private.

I hint at the issue gently in an email

I soften the feedback so much that the leadership urgency is lost.

I schedule a 1:1 and frame an alternative risk mitigation

I speak as a trusted peer and strategic partner, elevating both of our positions.

Pillar 4: Sponsorship & Alliance Building

Do you have executive sponsors advocating for you behind closed doors?

Mentors advise you; sponsors spend political capital to get you promoted when you are not in the room.

No, I rely strictly on my direct manager

I don't have relationships with senior leaders outside my immediate reporting line.

I have mentors, but no true sponsors

I receive advice and encouragement, but nobody actively puts their reputation on the line for my promotion.

Yes, I have multiple active executive sponsors

Senior VP and C-level leaders actively pull me into unlisted leadership opportunities.

Pillar 5: Compensation & Scarcity Mindset

How do you negotiate compensation offers or annual raises?

Navigating the immigrant instinct to be "grateful for the opportunity" vs demanding upper-quartile market equity.

I accept the initial offer gratefully

I feel pushback might make them revoke the offer or view me as ungrateful.

I ask politely for 5% to 10% more

I negotiate slightly on base salary, but leave executive equity and sign-on bonuses untouched.

I leverage multi-tier executive offer counter-strategies

I negotiate sign-on bonuses, accelerated equity vesting, and performance severance packages aggressively.

Pillar 6: Long-Term Leadership Horizon

What is your ultimate career trajectory goal?

Claiming your seat at the highest levels of global enterprise and public governance.

Senior Director / Principal Contributor

Maintain high compensation while managing a stable team.

Vice President / Head of Global Function

Own large enterprise P&L and orchestrate global organizational strategy.

C-Suite Officer & Corporate Board Director

Drive enterprise governance, shareholder value, and global transformation.
